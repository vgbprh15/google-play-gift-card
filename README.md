## The region question, and why it's the only error you can't undo

A Google Play balance is tied to the country of the account that redeems the code. Buy a US card for a UK account and the code simply won't redeem. Google's support documentation repeats the restriction in every version I checked, and nothing in it describes a process for moving a code between countries.

Skine's own checkout pushes the same point: “Google Play gift certificates come in different currencies and denominations for various regions. Be sure to select the right region when purchasing your Google Play e-gift card.”

How to check your account country before paying:

1. Open the Google Play Store app on your Android device.
2. Tap your profile picture, then **Settings**, then **General**, then **Account and device preferences**.
3. The country shown there is the one your balance belongs to. That is the region you select at checkout.

If you're buying for someone else, ask them to check the same screen. Their answer, not your shipping address or your phone's locale, determines which card works.

The second lock comes after redemption. Google's documentation is explicit that once a claim code has been redeemed, the balance cannot be transferred to another Google account. There is one narrow exception involving child accounts under family settings, handled separately. Practically: if you send a code to a friend's Gmail address and they redeem it, the money lives in their account. If they typed the wrong email or used a different Google account than you expected, you cannot pull it back.

## What you can't do once the balance is in your account

This is the part most buying guides skip, and it's the part that generates refund requests. Google's restrictions list is short and firm:

- **No cash value.** You may not sell or exchange a gift card for cash or any other prepaid payment method.
- **No commercial use.** Cards bought through Google Play can't be used for resale.
- **No card-buying-card.** You can't use a Google Play gift card to buy another gift card or certain ineligible items.
- **No Play Points on the purchase.** Digital gift cards are excluded from earning Google Play Points, except during specific promotions for eligible users. Play Pass promotions don't apply to gift card purchases either.
- **No changes after ordering.** The card type is fixed once the order goes through.

Google attaches a consequence to prohibited activity: prevention of redemption or action on the account, without a refund. That language is worth reading twice before buying a card for someone who plans to resell credit or trade it.

One more: if you were planning to pay for the card *with* your existing Google Play balance or Play Points, you can't. Google's current documentation says neither can be used to purchase Google Play gift cards or specialty gift cards. Older versions of the same help page said Play Points could buy specialty cards in some countries; the stricter wording is the one to plan around.

## FAQ

**Can I redeem a US card on a UK account?**
No. The card redeems only in the country where it was purchased.

**Can I move the balance to a different Google account later?**
Not after the code is redeemed. The money stays with the account that claimed it, apart from a child-account exception handled through family settings.

**Do Google Play gift codes expire?**
Skine's FAQ lists the question but doesn't publish an answer in the visible copy, and Google's restriction page doesn't give a blanket expiry date. Treat unused codes as something to redeem promptly and check the terms that come with your specific card.

**Should I buy for myself or as a gift?**
Buying for yourself is simpler: points accrue to your email, and the balance lands where you can use it. Gifting means confirming the recipient's account country first and accepting that the balance becomes theirs the moment they redeem.

**Do I earn Play Points on the card purchase?**
No. Digital gift cards are excluded from earning points outside specific promotions.
